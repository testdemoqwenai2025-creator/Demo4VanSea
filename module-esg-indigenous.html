Module D — Cascadia ESG & Indigenous Economic Integration

OCAP®-compliant Indigenous partnership directory. Cascadia-specific climate resilience scoring (Cascadia Subduction Zone, sea-level rise, wildfire, atmospheric river). Indigenous Data Governance Council.

Phase 2ESGOCAP®Indigenous Council

1 Purpose

Provide the ESG and Indigenous-engagement capabilities that distinguish Cascadia OS from any generic intelligence platform. This is where the regional moat is most visible — no global platform has the consented Indigenous-nation partnerships or the Cascadia-specific climate-risk data.

2 Key Features

2.1 Indigenous Partnership Directory

A verified, respectful, and OCAP®-compliant (Ownership, Control, Access, Possession) registry of Indigenous-owned businesses, with filters for specific nations and capabilities.

Refinement — formal OCAP® adoption

OCAP® is a registered trademark of the First Nations Information Governance Centre (FNIGC). The OS formally registers with FNIGC as an organization that respects OCAP®, documents OCAP® adoption in the data-governance policy, requires every Indigenous-owned business in the directory to opt in (no scraping, no inference), and honors the "Possession" principle by migrating to nation-controlled infrastructure (Year 2 target).

The directory is filterable by:

  • Nation — Muckleshoot, Squamish, Tsleil-Waututh, Musqueam, Tulalip, etc.
  • Capability — general contractor, electrical, plumbing, design, software, consulting
  • Set-aside eligibility — federal 8a, BC IBI, WA Indigenous preference, OR none
  • Capacity indicators — project size history, bonding capacity (only if consented)

2.2 Climate Resilience Scoring

Assets and supply chains are scored based on exposure to Cascadia-specific risks:

RiskSource
Cascadia Subduction Zone seismicUSGS National Seismic Hazard Map, site-specific amplification factors
Sea-level rise (port facilities)NOAA Sea Level Rise Viewer (US); BC Ministry of Forests (BC)
Wildfire corridorsUSGS Wildfire Risk to Communities; BC Wildfire Service
Atmospheric river floodNOAA Atlas 14 (US); Environment Canada IDF (BC)

The score is 0-100, where 100 = lowest risk. Each asset (factory, port, supplier facility) has a score. The user's supply chain has an aggregate score weighted by criticality.

Refinement — climate resilience scores are hash-anchored

The score is calculated from public data; the calculation is documented and reproducible; the score itself is hash-anchored in the provenance ledger so the user's ESG report cites a verifiable score. This is the "every fact carries provenance" principle in action.

3 The Indigenous Data Governance Council

Per the data-governance framework, the OS establishes a formal advisory board of First Nations and Tribal representatives with veto power over Indigenous-data features.

YearSeatsNations
Year 13Muckleshoot, Squamish, Suquamish or Tulalip
Year 25+ Confederated Tribes of Umatilla + BC Interior rotating
Year 3+7+ Tsleil-Waututh + another rotating

Authority: veto over Indigenous-data features, audit authority, budget authority, renewal authority.

Compensation: $5K USD per quarterly meeting per member ($20K/year), travel + admin support, ~$150K-$200K total annual budget. Compensation is non-negotiable — the "volunteer advisor" pattern is extractive.

First action: formally adopt OCAP® as the OS's data-governance framework for Indigenous-nation data.

4 Shared Economic Benefits

The OS commits to revenue-sharing and preference commitments for partner nations, made contractually enforceable in the partnership agreements:

  • Revenue share: 5% of OS subscription revenue from users who cite a specific Indigenous-nation partnership in their bid is paid to that nation (capped at $50K/year/nation in Year 1, uncapped Year 3+). The citing is logged in the bid's provenance record — the revenue-share calculation is auditable.
  • Capacity grants: $25K capacity-building grants to partner nations for technology training, data governance infrastructure, or youth STEM programs — directed by the Council.
  • Procurement preference: when the OS itself procures, partner-nation-owned businesses receive a 10% bid preference.

These are not "donations" — they are revenue-sharing and preference commitments.
