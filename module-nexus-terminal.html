Module A — Nexus Terminal

Bloomberg-Terminal-equivalent for Cascadia-specific financial and market intelligence. Command palette with syntax, cross-border arbitrage scanner, private market pulse.

Phase 2FinancialBloomberg-styleCross-border

1 Purpose

Provide a Bloomberg-Terminal-equivalent for Cascadia-specific financial and market intelligence. The user types a symbol or query, the OS surfaces live market data + cross-border context + private-market signals specific to the corridor.

Year 2 (Phase 2) launch target

Pricing: $499/user/month pro · $2,400/user/month team (5+ seats) · $6,000/month enterprise. Lower than Bloomberg ($2,400/mo) on price; higher on Cascadia-specific depth.

2 Key Features

2.1 Command Palette UI — the Bloomberg <GO> syntax

Press Cmd+K / Ctrl+K to open the command palette. Type AAPL <GO> for Apple market data, LUMBER FUTURES <GO> for the lumber futures chain, tender 2026-Q4-0142 <GO> to open a tender, diff WA CCA vs OR CPP to open the regulatory diff engine.

Refinement — accept both shortcut AND natural language

The parser accepts both AAPL <GO> AND show me Apple stock. Power users type the shortcut; new users type the question. The parser dispatches to the same handler. This eliminates the "Terminal is too complex" adoption risk without sacrificing the power-user experience.

2.2 Cross-Border Arbitrage Scanner

Real-time alerts on price discrepancies for cross-border commodities, factoring in:

  • Live FX rates (USD/CAD)
  • Border toll costs (Pacific Highway truck toll: $9.75 USD southbound, $13.50 CAD northbound)
  • USMCA regional value content thresholds
  • WA CCA cap-and-invest compliance cost
  • BC carbon tax ($80/tonne in 2026)
ALERT: Random-length lumber futures (CME) at $482 USD/MBF
       vs. Vancouver spot at $648 CAD/MBF.
       After FX (1 USD = 1.36 CAD), border toll, CCA compliance:
       gross arb = $14/MBF.
       Threshold: 2.5% gross arb (you set).
       ACTION: BUY Vancouver, SELL CME.

2.3 Private Market Pulse

Aggregated cap table data, SAFE note valuations, and hiring velocity metrics for Cascadia tech firms. Sources:

  • SEC Form D filings (US private placements — public record)
  • BCSC 45-106 filings (BC private placements — public record)
  • LinkedIn hiring velocity (company-page employee counts, rate-limited)
  • Consented alternative data (companies opt in for free OS access in exchange for their data being shown)
Refinement — what the Private Market Pulse does NOT include

Glassdoor salary data, LinkedIn individual-employee data. These are PII-adjacent with too-weak consent models. Stick to company-level aggregates.
