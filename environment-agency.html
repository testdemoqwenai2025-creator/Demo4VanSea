Climate Analysis — Expected vs Observed
Comparing past 30 days (observed 2026) to the same period in 2020 (climate normal year). Shows how current weather deviates from historical patterns. Data from Open-Meteo Archive API (ERA5 reanalysis, public, no key). OPEN-METEO ERA5
Methodology: "Expected" = average of daily values for the same date range (Aug 4 – Sep 2) from 2020, a recent climate-normal year. "Observed" = actual daily values for the past 30 days. Deviations >25% are flagged as anomalies. Snow days = days with >0mm snowfall. Rainy days = days with >0.1mm precipitation. Data source: ERA5 reanalysis via Open-Meteo Archive API (public, taxpayer-funded equivalent: NOAA Climate Data Online).

⚠ Cascadia Wildfire Outlook

Cascadia's wildfire risk has increased significantly over the past decade due to climate change, forest fuel accumulation, and expanding wildland-urban interface. Key trends:

  • WA: 2020 was worst season on record (1,250+ fires, 713k acres burned). 2023–2025 closer to 10-year average.
  • OR: 2020 Labor Day fires burned 1.2M acres — worst in state history. 2024–2025 above average.
  • BC: 2023 was worst season ever (2.84M hectares burned, 6x avg). 2025 also above average. Smoke impacts Seattle/Portland frequently.
  • Cross-border smoke: BC fires cause 60%+ of smoke days in Seattle/Portland. WA/OR fires cause 30%+ of smoke days in Vancouver/Victoria.

Sources: NIFC annual reports, WA DNR, OR ODF, BC Wildfire Service — all public, taxpayer-funded.

🦅 Migration Insights for Cascadia

Cascadia sits on two major migration corridors:

  • Pacific Flyway: Millions of birds migrate along the coast spring (Mar-May) and fall (Sep-Nov). Key stopovers: Skagit Valley, Boundary Bay, Ridgefield NWR.
  • Salmon runs: Chinook, coho, chum, sockeye return to natal streams Aug-Nov. Best viewing: Cedar River (Sept), Issaquah Salmon Hatchery (Oct), Adams River (Sockeye, every 4 years — next peak 2026).
  • Gray whale migration: ~20,000 whales pass Oregon/WA coast spring (northbound Mar-May) and winter (southbound Dec-Feb). Best viewing: Depoe Bay, Cape Flattery.
  • Elk rut: Sept-Oct. Bugling bulls in Olympic National Park, Mt Rainier NP, Oregon Coast Range.
  • Snow geese: 50,000+ winter in Skagit Valley Nov-March. Spectacular congregations at Fir Island.

Data: iNaturalist seasonal observations + USFWS migration reports + WA Dept of Fish & Wildlife.

📊 Budget Analysis & Uncertainties

Combined annual environmental spending in Cascadia: ~$10.3B (EPA share for Region 10 + state/provincial agencies). This equals roughly $250 per capita — above US national average ($180) but below leading European nations ($340).

Key uncertainties:

  • Wildfire costs: BC spent $1.1B emergency in 2023 — 8x base budget. Trending up 18% annually.
  • Federal IRA funding: $3B environmental justice at risk if administration changes. Represents 33% of EPA budget.
  • PFAS regulation: EPA estimating $1.5B compliance cost nationally; state share TBD.
  • Climate adaptation: WA Dept of Ecology requesting $200M for 2025-27 biennium (40% increase) for flood/wildfire resilience.
  • Cross-border data sharing: No formal US-Canada environmental data treaty; ad-hoc coordination only.

📊 Decade Outlook Analysis

The next decade in Cascadia will see $60+ billion in major infrastructure and environmental investment across the bioregion. Key themes:

  • Transportation transformation: Cascadia HSR ($24-42B) + I-5 Bridge ($10B) = potential $52B+ in cross-border mobility investment. HSR alone would generate estimated $355B economic return.
  • Clean energy buildout: PNW Hydrogen Hub ($1B federal) + BC North Coast Transmission ($2.8B CAD) + ongoing renewable builds = multi-billion clean energy transition.
  • Salmon restoration pivot: Columbia River programs ($66.7M/yr) + PSAR ($60.5M biennium) + Snake River dam study = potential $1.5B+ dam breaching decision looming.
  • Wildfire resilience scaling: WA ($500M/8yr) + BC ($220M CAD Budget 2026) = ~$700M+ dedicated wildfire resilience, but 2023 BC fires alone cost $1.1B emergency.
  • Indigenous-led conservation: Canada Guardians program ($340M CAD + $173M for Guardians) = largest Indigenous conservation investment in Canadian history.
  • Port modernization: Port of Seattle ($5.6B/5yr) + Port of Vancouver ($112M/yr) = sustained maritime infrastructure investment driving regional trade.
