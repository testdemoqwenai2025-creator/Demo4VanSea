1 The Explainability Dashboard

For every AI recommendation the OS surfaces, the user can click "Why?" to see the exact weighted factors. No black boxes.

Worked example

The AI suggests "85% match confidence on Tender #2026-Q4-0142." The user clicks "Why?" and sees the breakdown below.

2 What the Dashboard Does NOT Show

The dashboard does NOT show:

  • The model's training data (proprietary — competitors could replicate)
  • The model's weights (same reason)
  • Internal confidence intervals that aren't user-facing (debugging signals, not user-facing claims)

What it DOES show: the feature values used in this specific prediction, the weights applied, the composite score, the evidence chain for each feature, the provenance anchor.

The distinction

The user can audit the recommendation, not the model. This is the right balance between transparency and defensibility.

3 The "No Black-Box Decisions" Commitment

The OS commits: no AI recommendation is acted on autonomously on financial or legal decisions. Specifically:

ActionAI canAI cannot
Bid submissionDraft the bidSubmit the bid
Trade executionSuggest the tradeExecute the trade
Documentation modificationSuggest the changeModify without explicit per-edit approval

This is the "human-in-the-loop" pattern from the risk-mitigation strategy, made concrete. The AI is the drafter and advisor, not the submitter or executor.

4 Why This Is a Moat

The Explainability Dashboard, combined with the provenance ledger, makes the OS's recommendations auditable, not just explainable. A user can prove to a third party (auditor, contracting officer, lawyer) that the OS made a specific recommendation on a specific date with a specific reasoning — and that the reasoning was not tampered with after the fact.

Compounds annually

As AI-generated content floods the internet over the next 5-10 years, verifiable provenance becomes the scarcest resource. The OS will have years of accumulated provenance history that no competitor can fabricate.
