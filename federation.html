Federation — Sibling-Region OSes

Cascadia OS is the anchor regional OS. By Year 4-5, the OS template gets licensed to other bioregions. Salmon Nation first, Great Lakes second, Gulf of Mexico third, Northeast Corridor fourth.

1 What is Federation?

Cascadia OS is the anchor regional OS. By Year 4-5, the OS template gets licensed to other bioregions. Each sibling is independently operated — own team, own regional moat, own Indigenous-nation relationships — but shares the OS's infrastructure contracts (event bus protocol, provenance ledger cross-anchoring, data sovereignty framework).

Think of it like the Linux distribution model: Cascadia OS is upstream; sibling-region OSes are downstream distros that take the kernel and specialize for their region. The OS doesn't operate the siblings; it licenses the template to them.

Why federation, not expansion?

If Cascadia OS tried to be the Great Lakes OS, it would have to build relationships with the Anishinaabe, parse the Ontario Building Code, ingest the Michigan DOT tender feed — none of which transfer to Cascadia. Federation multiplies the moat instead of diluting it.

2 The Four Sibling-Region OSes

Each candidate is a North American bioregion — a region defined by geography + economic corridor + shared regulatory frameworks. The federation strategy is "start with the most aligned region, expand outward."

3 Federation Economic Model

When a sibling-region OS launches, it pays Cascadia OS:

Revenue share (ongoing)1%PerpetualOngoing license fee

In exchange, the sibling gets:

  • The architecture docs (14 documents in docs/architecture/)
  • The data-mesh reference implementation (e.g., services/tenders-ingestor/)
  • The provenance-ledger protocol (Ed25519 signing, monthly Merkle anchor to public blockchain)
  • The event-bus contract (versioned, schema-enforced)
  • The agent-stack reference (per docs/SKILLS.md)
  • The Indigenous Data Governance Council template (per docs/architecture/11-data-governance-ethical-framework.md §4)

The sibling owns its own implementation, its own team, its own regional moat. Cascadia OS doesn't own the sibling — it licenses the template.

4 What "First Federation" Actually Means

"Salmon Nation OS first" doesn't mean Cascadia OS operates Salmon Nation OS. It means:

  1. The Cascadia OS team publishes the architecture docs + reference implementation + federation protocol (already done — see the architecture page and the OS source repo)
  2. An independent team (most likely: a coalition of Pacific Northwest Indigenous-led tech nonprofits + a WA/OR state economic development office + a BC tech nonprofit) licenses the template
  3. That team stands up the Salmon Nation OS with their own funding (grant-funded + SaaS revenue from day 1)
  4. Cascadia OS gets a 2% revenue share for 5 years + a permanent Federation Council seat

This is the Linux-distribution model applied to regional-intelligence platforms. Cascadia is upstream; siblings are downstream distros.

5 Federation Council

By Year 5 (when Salmon Nation OS is operational), the Federation Council convenes.

SeatHolderTerm
Permanent seatCascadia OS (project lead)Permanent
Rotating seatEach operational sibling-region OS contributes one representative2-year terms (Salmon Nation from Year 5, Great Lakes from Year 7, Gulf from Year 9, Northeast from Year 12)
Indigenous-nation observerDesignated by the OS's Indigenous Data Governance Council; rotates among partner nations across federated regions1-year, non-voting

Cadence: Quarterly meetings; emergency convening on any cross-federation matter.

Authority (limited to cross-federation matters):

  • Event-bus protocol versioning (changes require Council ratification)
  • Provenance ledger cross-anchoring policy
  • Indigenous-nation data sovereignty framework updates (only with the Indigenous observer seat's consent)
  • Federation license terms (any change to the 2%/1% revenue share requires unanimous Council approval)

The Council does not have authority over individual OS operations. Each sibling OS is sovereign over its own region.
