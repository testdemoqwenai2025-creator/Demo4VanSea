Cascadia Weekly Economic Brief — Sep 29, 2026

1 BEA Q3 GDP: Seattle-Bellevue-Kent metro grew 3.2% QoQ bea.gov
The Seattle metro's $468B GDP grew 3.2% quarter-over-quarter, outpacing the national average of 2.1%. Tech sector drove 1.4 percentage points of growth. Cross-border trade with BC contributed 0.6pp — the first time cross-border trade has been separately quantified in BEA metro data. Combined Cascadia corridor GDP at $890B annualized, on track for $1T by 2028 per AI ensemble models.

2 BLS September employment: Cascadia tech employment +4,200 jobs bls.gov/cew
AI/ML led job creation with +2,100 positions, followed by CleanTech at +900. Seattle metro unemployment held at 3.8%; Vancouver at 4.2%. The corridor-wide talent gap persists: 12,000 unfilled tech positions, concentrated in AI/ML (4,200) and cloud infrastructure (3,100). H-1B approval rate for Cascadia employers ticked up to 78% (from 74% in Q2), reflecting USCIS processing improvements.

3 StatsCan bilateral trade: BC-WA trade reached $3.2B in September statcan.gc.ca
Bilateral trade between BC and Washington reached $3.2B in September, up 12.4% YoY. Tech exports drove the growth (+18.3%), with software + cloud services accounting for $1.1B. USMCA digital trade provisions are showing measurable impact: 23 fewer cross-border friction incidents reported by corridor tech firms (per Cascadia Innovation Corridor survey). The USMCA digital trade utilization rate is now estimated at 67% for Cascadia firms — up from 52% in 2024.
